About the Author

Vincent B. Troiano

A 36-year classroom educator whose writing centers on teacher voice, special education, individual instruction, and the effects of education reform on everyday classroom practice.

A career spent in classrooms

36 years teaching27 years special education9 years grades 4–6

Vincent B. Troiano taught for the Old Bridge Township School System in New Jersey for 36 years. He spent nine years teaching grades 4–6 and 27 years in special education, beginning in self-contained classes with students then identified as Emotionally Disturbed.

That long classroom career is the foundation of The Silent Voice in Education: The Importance of Involving Classroom Teachers. Troiano’s central concern is the distance that can develop between policy decisions and the day-to-day judgment required to teach individual students.

His writing draws on decades of classroom experience and focuses on the practical consequences of education policy for teachers and students.

The Silent Voice in Education

Published by Rowman & Littlefield on November 27, 2018, the book covers the 1960s reform era, local leadership, special education, classroom management, teacher evaluation, IEPs, local control, and individual instruction.
