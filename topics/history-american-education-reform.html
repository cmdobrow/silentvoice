Topic Guide

History of American Education Reform

Several arguments in The Silent Voice in Education begin with the reform era of the 1950s and 1960s. The historical material below provides context for several of the major movements discussed in the book and essays.

New Math and Cold War curriculum reform

ERIC’s historical study traces U.S. mathematics reform from 1951 into the late 1970s. Later scholarship describes how Cold War concerns and the launch of Sputnik helped accelerate support for new mathematics curricula.

The modern middle school movement

A scholarly historical overview indexed by ERIC identifies 1963–1979 as the beginning and formative period of the modern U.S. middle school movement. That history supports dating the movement to the same broad reform era discussed in Troiano’s book, without assuming that every local adoption had the same causes or effects.

Reading instruction and phonics

The history of reading instruction is more complex than any single program label. What can be stated with strong evidence is that the National Reading Panel found systematic phonics instruction significantly improves early reading outcomes and belongs within a complete reading program.

International comparisons

Simple claims that the United States was once “number one in the world” are not supported by the earliest major international math assessments. The First International Mathematics Study collected data in 1964; later analyses show U.S. performance was not at the top of the participating countries. Modern PISA results also vary by subject: in 2022, U.S. students were close to the OECD average in mathematics and above it in reading and science.
