Teacher Voice & Education Reform

The central argument of The Silent Voice in Education is that classroom teachers should have a meaningful role in decisions that shape instruction. This guide connects the site’s essays on reform history, mathematics curriculum, professional judgment, and local leadership.

Why teacher voice is the organizing idea

Troiano’s perspective comes from 36 years in public-school classrooms, including 27 years in special education. The question running through his work is not whether research, administration, or policy should disappear; it is how those systems can use evidence without excluding the practitioners who see the consequences of decisions every day.

The 2008 National Mathematics Advisory Panel is unusually relevant to that theme. Its recommendations say mathematically knowledgeable classroom teachers should have a central role in mathematics education and that instructional practice should draw both on high-quality research and on the professional judgment and experience of accomplished classroom teachers.
