Special Education & Individual Instruction

Vincent B. Troiano spent 27 of his 36 years in education teaching special education. That experience is a major part of The Silent Voice in Education and of his argument for greater respect for classroom judgment.

Vincent’s special education experience

Vincent taught in the Old Bridge Township School System in New Jersey for 36 years: nine years in grades 4–6 and 27 years in special education. His special education career began in self-contained classes working with students identified at the time as Emotionally Disturbed.

His writing emphasizes the difference between formal policy and what a teacher sees and decides from day to day while working with individual students.

Special education in The Silent Voice in Education

Several chapters are devoted directly to special education and individual instruction, including:

Special Education: Basis and Reform
Classroom Management in Special Education

The Teacher as Facilitator vs Goals and Objectives
Individual Instruction

The connection to teacher voice

Vincent uses his special education experience as part of the book’s broader case for classroom teachers having a meaningful voice in education. The recurring concern is the distance between systems, documents, and theories on one hand, and the teacher’s continuous knowledge of an individual student on the other.
